# Wi-Fi Is Connected but There’s No Internet: How to Fix It

If your phone, laptop, or tablet says it is connected to Wi-Fi but websites and apps will not load, your device may be connected to the router without having a working path to the internet. The fastest way to narrow down the cause is to connect a second device to the same Wi-Fi network. If that device also cannot get online, investigate the router, modem, or internet service. If other devices work, focus on the affected device.

Follow the checks below in order. Stop when your connection works again. Avoid factory-resetting your router or resetting all network settings until simpler checks have failed.

## First, find out whether one device or every device is affected

A device joining Wi-Fi confirms that it has connected to the local wireless network. It does not, by itself, prove that the router has a working internet connection. The fault may be between the router and your internet provider, or in the settings of one device.

### Test another device on the same Wi-Fi

Use a second phone, computer, or tablet and connect it to the same Wi-Fi network. Try opening more than one website or app.

### Try the affected device on a different Wi-Fi network

If possible, connect the affected phone or computer to another trusted Wi-Fi network. You can also use a personal hotspot briefly, keeping mobile-data charges in mind.

| What you find | What it suggests | Where to go next |
|---|---|---|
| No device on your home Wi-Fi can get online | The issue may involve the router, modem, service line, or ISP | Check for an outage, inspect equipment and cables, then restart the network equipment |
| Other devices work, but one device does not | The issue may be limited to that device's saved network, settings, software, or adapter | Restart the device, reconnect to Wi-Fi, then follow the relevant Windows, Android, or iPhone steps |
| The affected device works on another Wi-Fi network | The device can connect elsewhere; investigate the original router or network settings | Check the home network and contact its administrator or ISP if needed |
| The affected device fails on several different Wi-Fi networks | The device itself may have a configuration, software, or hardware problem | Check device settings and contact the device maker if basic steps fail |
| Only one app or website fails, while other internet services work | This may be an app, website, account, DNS, VPN, or firewall issue rather than a complete Wi-Fi failure | Test other sites and apps; check the affected service or app |

These tests narrow down the likely location of the problem. They do not identify the exact cause on their own.

## If every device is connected but has no internet

When several devices on the same home network cannot get online, start with the shared connection rather than changing settings on every phone or computer.

### Check for an internet service outage

Use mobile data or another internet connection to check your internet provider's official service-status page or app. If your provider reports an outage in your area, local troubleshooting may not restore service. Wait for the provider's update or contact support if the outage page does not explain the problem.

A third-party outage-reporting site can show that other people are reporting trouble, but it is not a substitute for confirmation from your ISP.

### Check the modem, router, and cables

Your home setup may have a separate modem and Wi-Fi router, or one combined gateway device. Check the equipment you actually have.

- Make sure the modem, router, or gateway has power.
- Check that the cable carrying the internet service is firmly connected.
- If you use a separate modem and router, check the Ethernet cable between them.
- Look for warning indicators, but use the equipment manual to interpret them. Light colors and blinking patterns vary by model and provider.
- If a cable or power adapter appears damaged, do not keep using it. Contact the equipment provider or a qualified technician.

### Restart the modem and router

A power restart can make the equipment establish a fresh connection. It is different from a factory reset and should not erase your Wi-Fi name or password.

If you have separate modem and router devices:

1. Unplug the router's power.
2. Unplug the modem's power. If it has a backup battery, follow the manufacturer's instructions.
3. Wait at least 30 seconds.
4. Plug the modem back in and allow it to finish starting up.
5. Plug the router back in and wait for it to reconnect.
6. Test the connection on a device.

If you have one combined modem/router gateway, restart that single device according to its manual or your ISP's instructions. Everyone using the network will temporarily lose the connection during the restart.

Microsoft's [Windows Wi-Fi troubleshooting guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows) also recommends restarting network equipment and notes that a modem/router combination device only needs to be restarted as one unit.

### Contact your internet provider if the whole network remains offline

Contact your ISP when multiple devices still cannot access the internet after you have checked the service status, power, and connections. Tell support:

- Whether every device is affected.
- Whether the modem/router shows an error indicator, using the exact label or pattern from its manual.
- Whether you restarted the equipment and when.
- Whether a wired Ethernet connection works, if you have a computer and cable available.

Do not factory-reset the router just because the internet is down. The cause may be outside your home, and a factory reset can erase settings needed to reconnect.

## If only one device cannot access the internet

If other devices work on the same Wi-Fi, focus on the affected phone, tablet, or computer.

### Restart the device and reconnect to Wi-Fi

Restart the device, make sure Airplane Mode is off, and check that Wi-Fi is enabled. Turn Wi-Fi off and on once, then reconnect to your network.

If the device still shows no internet, you can forget the saved network and join it again. Before doing this, make sure you know the Wi-Fi password.

- **Windows 11:** Open Settings, choose Network & internet, select Wi-Fi, then Manage known networks. Choose your network and select Forget. Reconnect and enter the password.
- **iPhone or iPad:** Open Settings, tap Wi-Fi, tap the information button beside the network, and choose Forget This Network. Rejoin the network.
- **Android:** Open Settings and find Network & internet or Connections, then Wi-Fi. Menu names vary by device maker and Android version. Select the saved network and choose Forget, then reconnect.

Microsoft documents the Windows steps in its [official Wi-Fi guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows). Apple's [Wi-Fi connection guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111786) explains the iPhone and iPad checks. Google's [Android connection guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2651367) recommends restarting the device and comparing Wi-Fi with mobile data.

### Check the right settings for your device

**Windows 10 or 11**

- Confirm that Wi-Fi is on and Airplane Mode is off.
- If you use a manual proxy, VPN, or security product, check whether it is required by your workplace or school before changing it.
- Windows 11 users can run the Network and Internet troubleshooter in the Get Help app.
- If the problem began after a driver or Windows update, check the computer maker's support instructions before removing a network driver.

**Android**

Google recommends switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data to compare the connections. Turn Wi-Fi off and check whether mobile data works, then turn mobile data off and test Wi-Fi again. If mobile data has no signal, move to an area with better cellular coverage or contact your mobile provider. If mobile data works but Wi-Fi does not, continue troubleshooting the Wi-Fi network.

Android menu labels differ across manufacturers, so use your phone maker's instructions for advanced settings. Google's [official Android guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2651367) covers the basic checks, Wi-Fi signal, Airplane Mode, and router restart.

**iPhone or iPad**

- Open Settings and tap Wi-Fi. Confirm Wi-Fi is on and that a blue checkmark appears beside the network.
- Make sure Airplane Mode is off.
- If the Wi-Fi setting is greyed out, restart the device.
- If you use VPN or security software, follow Apple's guidance and consult the provider or your organization's administrator before removing managed software.
- Apple also identifies Connectivity Assist as a setting to check during troubleshooting. Its behavior and availability can vary by software version and region.

See Apple's [iPhone and iPad Wi-Fi troubleshooting steps](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111786) for the current instructions.

## Advanced checks when the basic steps do not work

Advanced network changes are not necessary for every “connected, no internet” problem. Use them only when the symptoms point to a device configuration issue, and follow instructions for your operating system.

### Check the IP address on Windows

Windows normally receives a local IP address from the router through DHCP when the network is configured to obtain an address automatically. Microsoft's support guide notes that an address beginning with 169.254 can indicate that Windows did not obtain an address from the router in that setup.

To inspect the address:

1. Open Command Prompt.
2. Enter ipconfig and press Enter.
3. Find the Wi-Fi adapter and review its IPv4 address and Default Gateway.

Do not change a static IP or DHCP setting unless you understand how the network is configured. Work, school, and managed networks may use settings chosen by an administrator.

### Use a gateway ping to test the local router connection

This is an advanced Windows check. It tests whether the computer can communicate with its local network gateway; it does not test the entire internet connection.

1. In Command Prompt, run ipconfig.
2. Find the Default Gateway address under the Wi-Fi adapter.
3. Enter ping followed by that address. For example: ping 192.168.1.1.
4. Review whether replies are returned.

A reply indicates that the computer can reach the gateway. If websites still do not load, the issue may be beyond the local Wi-Fi link, such as the modem, router's internet connection, or ISP. If there is no reply, investigate the local Wi-Fi/router connection or ask the device/network administrator for help. Some networks may block or deprioritize ping, so a failed ping alone is not definitive proof of a faulty router.

Microsoft explains this test and its limitations in its [official Windows troubleshooting instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows).

### Check DNS only after basic connectivity checks

DNS helps a device find the network address associated with a website name. A DNS problem can affect browsing even when the device is connected to Wi-Fi. However, changing DNS will not repair an ISP outage, a disconnected cable, or a router that has lost its upstream connection.

If only certain websites fail, or the device can reach the local gateway but cannot resolve website names, check the DNS settings for your operating system or ask your network administrator. Avoid copying random DNS addresses or changing managed network settings without approval.

### Leave driver removal and network reset until later

If a Windows connection stopped working after a driver update, consult the computer manufacturer's instructions. Microsoft advises having a backup network driver available before uninstalling the current adapter driver, because you may need another computer and a USB drive to reinstall it if the affected PC has no internet access.

Windows Network reset should be a last resort. Microsoft says it removes installed network adapters and resets their settings. VPN clients and virtual switches may need to be reinstalled or configured again afterward. Read Microsoft's [Network reset guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows) before using it.

## What not to reset too soon

The word “reset” can describe actions with very different consequences.

| Action | What it does | What you may need to do afterward | When to consider it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power restart | Turns the modem/router or device off and back on | Wait for it to reconnect | An early, low-disruption check |
| Forget and rejoin one Wi-Fi network | Removes that saved network profile from the device | Enter the Wi-Fi password again | When one device has trouble rejoining a network |
| Reset device network settings | Removes saved network configurations and may reset other network-related settings | Rejoin Wi-Fi and reconfigure affected settings | Only after simpler device checks fail |
| Factory-reset the router | Erases the router's configuration and restores factory defaults | Set up Wi-Fi and any required ISP settings again | Last resort, preferably with ISP or manufacturer guidance |

Apple warns that Reset Network Settings removes saved Wi-Fi networks and passwords, as well as cellular, VPN, and APN settings. If a school or business manages the device or network, consult its administrator before resetting. A router factory reset is different again: it can erase the Wi-Fi name, password, and provider-specific configuration. Do not press the router's physical reset button unless you are prepared to set it up again.

## When to contact your ISP, device maker, or network administrator

| Situation | Who to contact |
|---|---|
| Multiple devices cannot get online through your home network, and basic equipment checks do not help | Your internet provider |
| Your provider confirms an outage or reports a line/service fault | Your internet provider |
| One device fails on several different Wi-Fi networks | The device maker or a qualified technician |
| A work or school device uses managed VPN, proxy, or network settings | Your IT or network administrator |
| Router lights or connection status indicate a fault you cannot identify from the manual | Your ISP or router manufacturer |

When you contact support, share what you tested and the results. That helps avoid repeating steps and gives the support team a clearer starting point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my Wi-Fi say connected but there is no internet?

Your device may be connected to the local Wi-Fi router, while the router or internet service is not providing access to the wider internet. The problem can also be limited to one device. Test another device on the same Wi-Fi to narrow it down.

### Why does my phone say connected to Wi-Fi but no internet?

If other devices work on the same Wi-Fi, restart your phone, check Airplane Mode, and reconnect to the saved network. On Android, compare Wi-Fi with mobile data. On iPhone, confirm Wi-Fi is enabled and check Apple's recommended settings.

### Why does my laptop say connected but no internet?

If other devices can browse on the same network, check the laptop's Wi-Fi settings, saved network profile, VPN or proxy configuration, and network adapter. On Windows, the built-in troubleshooter and Microsoft's gateway ping steps can help narrow down the issue.

### Why do all my devices show Wi-Fi connected but no internet?

A problem with the router, modem, service line, or ISP may be affecting the whole network. Check your provider's outage status, inspect equipment and cables, and restart the modem/router according to its manual.

### Should I reset my router if Wi-Fi has no internet?

Start with a power restart, not a factory reset. A factory reset erases the router's configuration and may require you to set up the Wi-Fi network and ISP settings again. Use it only when other steps fail and you have the setup information or guidance from your provider.
